- Original article: [WTF Is a Loop? Peter Steinberger vs. Boris Cherny](https://x.com/i/article/2063850827694096385)

## One-line summary

This piece argues that AI coding is shifting from "writing better prompts" to "designing and verifying agent loops."

## Key takeaways

- The article defines a loop not as simple repetition, but as a system where the model acts, reads the result, and updates its judgment.
- It traces the idea through ReAct, AutoGPT, ralph, /goal, and /loop, showing how the meaning of loop has expanded over time.
- In 2026, a loop is no longer just a single agent running; it also includes higher-level orchestration that supervises other loops.
- The real bottleneck is feedback and verification. Without self-checks, loops can easily become infinite and expensive.
- That means the cost center has shifted from tokens to the work of managing and stopping the loop.
- The most durable asset is not the loop itself, but the reusable **skills** it invokes.

## Notable angle

- The important person is no longer the one who writes the best prompt, but the one who decides what should repeat and when it should stop.
- As automation gets stronger, human value moves upward toward defining judgment criteria and stop conditions.
- The stronger the loop, the stronger the verification needs to be.

## Why it matters

This article reframes AI coding as an ongoing operational system rather than a one-off productivity trick.

For daily notes and knowledge building, the same lesson applies: what matters is not just producing more, but deciding what to keep, what to cut, and where to stop.

## Follow-up notes

- Any loop should include both a verification step and a clear stop condition.
- Repetition is convenient, but unmanaged repetition becomes a cost and a risk.
- I am likely to ask "what is worth looping?" before "what can I automate?"
- In short, AI-era skill is drifting closer to **loop design** than prompt writing.
