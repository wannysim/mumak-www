- [When AI starts to make itself: progress toward recursive self-improvement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/when-ai-starts-to-make-itself)
- GeekNews summary: [AI가 스스로를 만들 때: 재귀적 자기 개선을 향한 우리의 진전](https://news.hada.io/topic?id=30189)

## One-line summary

Anthropic argues that AI is moving beyond writing code for us and into doing experiments, building infrastructure, and supervising training itself — raising the question of whether that loop can eventually close into recursive self-improvement (RSI).

## Key takeaways

- The length of tasks models can reliably complete appears to be doubling about every four months.
- The range has expanded from roughly 4-minute software tasks in March 2024 to 12-hour tasks by 2026.
- As of May 2026, more than 80% of code merged into Anthropic's codebase is written by Claude.
- The amount of code merged by a typical engineer per day is also up to around 8× the 2024 level.
- Claude has become very strong at clearly defined experimental execution, but _research judgment_ — deciding what matters most — still belongs to humans.
- The future seems to split into three broad paths:
  - current progress slowing down
  - hybrid efficiency gains where humans set direction and AI scales execution
  - full recursive self-improvement
- Slowing things down safely would require verifiable coordination across frontier labs in multiple countries.

## Notable angle

- This piece reframes AI progress as a question of _how far the task loop can close_, not just whether the chatbot is better.
- Coding agents are already moving from writing code to helping create the next model.
- Even now, the bottleneck is less about generation and more about _judgment, prioritization, and problem selection_.

## Why it matters

This is more than a tech news item; it is a very clear snapshot of how much autonomy AI might gain next.

For me, as someone who keeps building daily notes, blog content, and a knowledge archive, it also raises a more practical question: not just _what AI can do faster_, but _which decisions should still stay with a human_.

## Follow-up notes

- At this stage, AI is strong at accelerating execution, but humans still need to own problem definition and prioritization.
- It will probably become more important to write down both _what to build_ and _what not to build_.
- Even if RSI becomes possible, safety and coordination mechanisms need to grow with it.
- In daily archives, it may be worth recording not only technical progress itself, but also where I choose to delegate judgment and where I keep it.
