It took me two months after quitting swimming to realize what I had lost.
I thought I was giving up one form of exercise, but I had also given up the rhythm that brought my day to a close.

## When I Stopped Swimming, My Evenings Stopped Ending

I quit swimming after six months.
The reason was time.
Going to the pool at 9 p.m. after work felt as if it took away everything that remained of my day.

I liked swimming itself.
Simply getting into the water made me feel better.
But evening sessions took up too much time, while the morning sessions I tried instead moved too fast for me.
Once, after a morning session, I even found myself dry-heaving in the shower.
Neither option was something I could sustain.

About two months after I stopped, my body began to feel different.
My stamina was lower, I no longer felt as well as I had, and I was going to bed later and later.
Without the pool to go to, I stayed up on my computer or looked at my phone late into the night.
Going to bed late left me sluggish the next day, and feeling sluggish made me even less inclined to move.
I would lie down with my phone and end up going to bed late again.

At first, I thought people had to use up all their remaining energy through exercise before they could fall asleep from exhaustion.
Research does suggest that exercise can improve sleep in adults with insomnia, but studies differ on what kind of exercise, at what time, and in what amount works best.
Reducing my experience to a single rule about energy expenditure did not explain enough.
([Riedel et al., _The Effect of Physical Exercise Interventions on Insomnia_](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.smrv.2024.101948))

For me, swimming was less about expending energy than marking the end of the day.
Going to the pool, moving my body, showering, and returning home formed a sequence that brought the evening to a close.
When I quit swimming, I lost not only the exercise but also that rhythm.

I knew I should start exercising again, but I could not quite bring myself to begin.
Once again, I blamed a lack of willpower.
Looking back, however, the problem had more to do with my environment than my resolve.
Evening swimming demanded too much time, and morning swimming demanded an intensity I could not handle.
What I needed was not stronger motivation, but a routine I could sustain.

## Not a Question of Character, but of Practice

In much the same way, I used to interpret my awkwardness with people as a problem with my character.
I do not mean that I thought I lacked morals.
I mean that I struggled to consider others first, express gratitude for kindness, and communicate my intentions in a way another person could understand.
Lately, I have often felt less capable at these things than my colleagues.

Why did this feel awkward to me while it came naturally to someone else?
As I thought about it, I began to see a difference in experience.
Because of my temperament and lifestyle, I do not meet many people.
I tend to spend time with the same familiar people, and as we grow comfortable with one another, I become less careful.
Familiarity makes me less deliberate in what I say and do.
That habit carries over when I deal with strangers or colleagues as well.

For a long time, I described this as a flaw in my character.
But character is too large a word.
Being slow to express gratitude, failing to ask about another person's situation first, or neglecting to reconsider an explanation from their point of view are not verdicts on who I am.
They are specific behaviors that I can change through practice.

Reflection matters.
But thinking alone for a long time does not automatically produce a different response the next time.
I need to say thank you, ask one more question before reaching a conclusion, and pay attention to how my words land by watching the other person's response.
Relationship skills can only be practiced within relationships.

I do not think that simply meeting more people will improve those skills.
Without paying attention, I may only repeat the same behavior more often.
What matters more than the number of people is choosing a specific behavior to practice.
It needs to be something I can observe: expressing gratitude on the day I receive help, asking before assuming another person's intentions, or choosing my words more carefully with the people I feel closest to.

## An Environment That Can Change Me

Exercise and relationships may seem like separate subjects.
But in both, I was making the same mistake.
When I failed to keep exercising, I thought I lacked willpower; when I acted awkwardly in a relationship, I thought I lacked character.
Then I waited to become more determined or a better person.

Looking back, self-reproach changed neither situation.
What I lacked were the conditions that would let me practice again and again.
I did not stop swimming because I had failed to make up my mind firmly enough.
I had chosen times and levels of intensity that did not fit my life.
In relationships, too, I needed chances to choose specific behaviors and practice them in real situations.

Creating an environment does not mean pushing myself into the most difficult situation possible.
An environment I cannot handle, like those morning swimming sessions, can prevent repetition altogether.
What I need is not an environment I can endure once, but one I can return to again and again.

For exercise, that means finding a time and intensity I can sustain without giving up my entire evening.
For relationships, it means making room in everyday life to practice the behaviors I want to develop, rather than vaguely trying to meet more people.
That setting might be a hobby group or a regular commitment.

Some things do not change simply because I understand them in my head.
A bodily rhythm must be built through movement, and relationship skills have to be learned among other people, through mistakes and correction.
I do not need to become a person of great willpower or a better person first.
I can begin by creating an environment where I can keep practicing.

Perhaps what changes me is not a grand resolution, but an environment that lets me keep showing up and trying again.
