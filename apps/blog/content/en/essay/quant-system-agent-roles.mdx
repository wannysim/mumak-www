I have been building a quant investment system with Hermes.
It applies investment rules to market data, records the results, and gives me something to work from when considering the next strategy.
It is currently paper trading with observed US market prices and virtual funds.

The system has grown quite large.
From researching strategies to collecting prices and recording transactions, each addition seemed understandable on its own. Looking at the whole system, though, I started losing track of what happened where.
Rather than add another feature, I wanted to write down who does what.

## Not every task needed the same AI

Model usage was one of my first concerns when dividing the roles.
For a system running on weekdays, I wondered whether strategy research and document summaries really needed the same model.
I wanted to give research enough room for reasoning and leave well-defined tasks to a lighter model.

But before choosing between models, there was another distinction to make.
Some tasks did not need AI at all.

Collecting prices, calculating quantities from fixed allocations, and updating the ledger already have defined rules.
There was no reason to explain those rules to AI and ask for an answer every time.
Code was the better place for calculations that should produce the same result from the same input.

I decided to use AI for strategy research and problem analysis, while ordinary programs handle recurring operations.
Code also produces the daily report by putting ledger values into a fixed format.
AI becomes useful when looking at those records and considering what to change.

## Research and operational support

I divided the AI work between Astra, Sol, and Luna.
These are not three separate investment bots.
They are different models called for particular jobs within the same system.

Astra handles strategy research.
It takes on work that needs more thought, such as choosing investment ideas to test and interpreting experimental results.
A promising return needs examination: did it depend on a particular period, and does it hold up after trading costs?
Astra is also the model I use in the main research conversation to discuss direction.

Sol helps with operations.
It can read logs to narrow down a price-collection failure or handle a bounded code change and its tests.
I also made it the default model for delegated work.
It does not decide whether to buy or sell each time a trade comes up.
Code executes the established trading rules; Sol is called when something needs investigation or a change.

Luna organizes material.
It extracts facts, sources, and dates from supplied documents for the next task.
I ask it to keep the material concise and accurate rather than add new investment judgments.
It also handles small tasks such as generating session titles.
Sorting CSV files and adding up profit and loss still stay in code.

The division follows the work more than the model names.
I choose where to send a task based on whether it needs deeper reasoning, bounded execution, or document organization.

## Work passes through the main conversation

I send requests to Hermes through Telegram.
Hermes breaks down the work, passes relevant material to a role, and brings the results back.
It also runs programs on schedule and delivers reports.

The three agents are not always running and debating with one another.
The main conversation delegates only the work that is needed.
Instead of handing over the entire conversation, it passes the task's purpose, relevant material, and boundaries on what should not be changed.

For a price-collection problem, a request to Sol might look like this:

> Read these logs and narrow down the collection failure. Leave the ledger alone and suggest what to check next.

Sol returns observed facts, possible causes, and check results to the main conversation.
The actual logs or test results are checked before deciding on the next task.
For research that needs some preparation, material organized by Luna can become input for Astra's review.
Not every task calls all three models in sequence.

I wanted the results to be something I could check.
An agent saying a task is complete does not necessarily mean the work is done.
The changed files and test results need to be checked before continuing.

The overall structure looks like this:

```text
Me: investment direction and operating rules
  |
  v
Hermes: hand off tasks and check results
  +-- Astra: strategy research
  +-- Sol: operational checks and problem analysis
  +-- Luna: organize material
  |
  v
Reviewed strategy and rules
  |
  v
Code: collect prices -> calculate signals and sizes -> check risk
  |
  v
Paper fills -> ledger -> report
  |
  +-- Review the results in the next round of research
```

## What runs daily, and what changes next

Code runs when the market opens.
It checks trading dates and session times, collects prices, and calculates signals and quantities using the fixed strategy.
Risk checks come before paper fills and ledger updates.
This process and the template-based daily report are scheduled jobs that do not call an LLM.

The current experiment uses a low-frequency trend strategy.
It does not need to trade every day, and no-trade days are recorded too.
Results are grouped into a report scheduled for 09:00 Korea time.
Since US trading often overlaps with my sleep, I chose to batch routine fill notifications and receive urgent problems separately.

I set up month-end reviews to revisit the strategy using the accumulated records.
The intermediate review is assigned to Sol and the final strategy review to Astra.
Rather than look only at profit and loss, I want to consider costs and operational problems when deciding what to test next.

Experiments with other strategies use separate ledgers.
Mixing them with the current strategy's records would make it harder to tell which change affected the results.
I also built a connection for applying the same strategy to an independent paper path alongside future live trading, so execution and cost differences can be compared.
Live trading is not enabled yet.

## A system I can keep understanding

I started out thinking mainly about building a quant investment system.
As I wrote down the structure, I realized that I was also putting a way of delegating to AI into practice.
Some work gets a deeper review, some is broken into small tasks, and established rules are left to code to repeat.

Dividing the roles does not remove my need to understand the whole system.
I still need to know where a result came from and which changes will affect the next run.
I do not want it to grow into something I can no longer explain.

For now, I want to keep this process running rather than add more features.
As records accumulate, I expect to see places where things differ from what I anticipated.
I want to keep the system understandable enough to decide which rules to retain and what to research again when that happens.
