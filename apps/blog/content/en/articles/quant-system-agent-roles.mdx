I have been building a quant investment system with Hermes. It applies a set of investment rules to market data, records the results, and gives me something to work from when planning the next strategy. It is currently paper trading with observed US market prices and virtual funds.

One part I spent time on was deciding what to leave to AI. A single model did not need to handle everything from strategy research to daily calculations. I assigned research to Astra, operational support to Sol, and document work to Luna, while keeping recurring operations in code.

## Not everything needs a model call

Model usage mattered because this system would run on weekdays. I wanted enough reasoning for a substantial strategy review, but there was little reason to call a model for a return calculation or ledger update whose rules were already defined.

I started by separating two kinds of work:

- AI helps form hypotheses, interpret results, and investigate problems.
- Code processes data, calculates quantities, and keeps records under fixed rules.

The same applies to the daily report. Putting ledger values into a template is a job for an ordinary program. A model becomes useful when interpreting those results and considering what to change next.

## The overall structure

```text
Me: investment direction and operating rules
  |
  v
Hermes: divide the work and bring results together
  +-- Astra: strategy research and important judgments
  +-- Sol: operational checks and problem analysis
  +-- Luna: organize supplied material
  |
  v
Reviewed strategy and rules
  |
  v
Code: collect prices -> calculate signals and sizes -> check risk
  |
  v
Paper fills -> ledger -> daily report
  |
  +-- Results feed the next round of research
```

I discuss direction with Hermes through Telegram. Hermes breaks down the work, assigns it to the appropriate role, and brings the results back together. It also handles scheduled program runs and report delivery.

The three roles are closer to people I can call on for a particular job than separate investment bots. The account connection and order path stay in the existing execution service.

## What each agent does

### Astra researches strategies

Astra handles investment ideas, experiment design, and interpretation. It is also the model for the main research conversation.

When a strategy looks promising, its job is to examine whether the result depends too much on a particular period or changes once trading costs are included. I reserved it for work that needs more thought, such as deciding what to test next and which assumptions to revisit.

### Sol helps with operations

Sol handles well-defined tasks and operational investigations. If price collection fails, it can read the relevant logs and narrow down the cause. If code needs a change, it can work on a separate copy and test it. Sol is also the default model for delegated workers.

It does not make a new decision for every trade. Code executes the trading rules; Sol is called when an investigation is needed. Rather than freely changing the original ledger or operating rules, it returns its work to the main conversation for review before anything is applied.

### Luna organizes material

Luna extracts facts, sources, and dates from supplied documents. Its job is to turn longer material into something manageable for the next decision.

If an announcement says revenue increased without giving a percentage, Luna should summarize what is known rather than guess the growth rate. It also handles small auxiliary tasks such as session titles. Sorting a CSV or adding up profit and loss stays in code.

## How work moves between agents

The main conversation coordinates the work. The agents do not all share the entire conversation and debate continuously. The coordinator selects a role and passes along the task and relevant material.

For example, a price-collection problem could be handled like this:

```text
Main conversation -> Sol
  "Read these logs and narrow down the collection failure.
   Leave the ledger alone and suggest the next check."

Sol -> Main conversation
  Return observed facts, possible causes, and check results

Main conversation
  Check the actual logs and tests, then decide the next task
```

For research, material organized by Luna can become input for Astra's review. But there is no daily sequence that calls all three models in turn. I delegate only the work needed at the time.

Each role has a defined set of tools and limits on time and turns. Outputs and execution records are kept as well. An agent saying "done" is not the end of a task: files and test results need to be checked. That gives whoever picks up the next task a clearer basis for continuing.

## Connecting daily operations to the next experiment

Ordinary code handles the recurring work when the market opens. It checks trading dates and session times, collects prices, and calculates signals and quantities from the fixed strategy. Risk checks come before paper fills and ledger updates. This path and the template-based daily report run as `no_agent` scheduled jobs, without calling an LLM.

The current experiment uses a low-frequency trend strategy, so it does not need to trade every day. No-trade days are recorded too, and results are grouped into a report scheduled for 09:00 Korea time. Since US trading often overlaps with my sleep, I chose to batch routine fill notifications and receive urgent problems separately.

At month-end, those records become material for another strategy review. The intermediate review is assigned to Sol and the final strategy review to Astra. The idea is to consider costs and operational problems alongside profit and loss, then decide what to test next.

I also separated the purposes of paper trading. The current paper episode observes the existing strategy, while experiments with different rules use separate ledgers. I built a connection for applying the same strategy to an independent paper path alongside future live trading, so execution and cost differences can be compared. Live trading is not enabled yet.

For now, I want to keep this process running rather than add more models. Code handles the repetitive work, AI concentrates on research and problem-solving, and I use the results to choose the next direction. I am building the system on my own, but dividing the work means I do not have to hold every part of it in my head at once.
