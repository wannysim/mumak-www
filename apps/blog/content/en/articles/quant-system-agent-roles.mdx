The quant system I built does not ask an AI to decide every buy and sell. AI helps research strategies and investigate operational problems. Ordinary code collects data, calculates quantities, checks risk conditions, and maintains the ledger under fixed rules.

According to the implementation records dated September 21, 2026, the system is in forward paper trading using observed US market prices. A connection for running live and paper tracks together has also been implemented, but live-account activation remains closed pending separate verification and approval. This is an account of how people and agents divide the work, rather than a report on investment performance.

## Separate repetitive work before assigning models

Calling an expensive model every time a weekday system runs would consume too much of the usage allowance. Sending every important strategy review to a lighter model did not seem right either.

The initial division was simple: Astra for deep research, Sol for operations, and Luna for organizing material. Before assigning models, though, I needed to identify **the work that does not need a model at all**.

There is little reason to use natural-language reasoning for a defined return calculation, position sizing rule, or ledger update. Those operations should be code whose inputs and outputs can be checked. A daily report can also be generated without a model when it places verified ledger values into a fixed template.

I kept normal recurring operations in code and reserved model calls for work that needs interpretation or judgment. This limits model use, but it also helps apply the same rules consistently and trace problems when they occur.

## The structure: verification between research and execution

```text
User: investment goals, capital, risk limits, final approval
  |
  v
Main Hermes conversation: scope work, select roles, verify results
  +-- Astra: strategy hypotheses, bias, and risk review
  +-- Sol: operational exceptions and clearly scoped tasks
  +-- Luna: facts, sources, and dates in supplied material
  |
  v
Strategy and settings fixed after verification and approval
  |
  v
Ordinary code: collect -> calculate signals and sizes -> check risk
  |
  v
Current paper fills -> ledger and valuation -> daily report
  |
  +-- Observations feed later research and month-end review
```

These are logical roles. They are not three AIs with separate brokerage accounts or separate servers holding a continuous conversation. Assigning the roles did not create new brokerage connections or copies of financial credentials.

Hermes handles the conversation, task delegation, scheduled execution, and delivery of results. Financial execution stays behind the existing integrated execution service. Adding agents does not mean duplicating order permissions.

## Astra: scrutinizing the strategy

Astra handles strategy hypotheses, experiment design, bias and overfitting, and important risk judgments. The main research conversation also remains on Astra.

The aim of strategy research is to find worthwhile returns, but a review cannot stop at the return figure. It has to ask whether a rule benefited from a particular period, used information unavailable at the time, or loses its appeal after costs. A good result from a short paper run should not become a claim of lasting advantage.

Astra produces hypotheses, evidence, proposed experiments, and an account of uncertainty. Its natural-language answer is not an operating policy. Strategy changes need verification and confirmation, and a move to live trading requires separate user approval.

## Sol: narrowing operational problems within an approved scope

Sol handles clearly bounded delegated tasks and operational exceptions. For example, it can inspect a collection failure, distinguish a provider problem from an input-format problem, and propose the next safe check.

An operations role is not an order-placement role. The execution code carries out the predefined trading rules. A Sol call through the role runner follows a policy against modifying the financial executor, operating policy, or original ledger. If a code change is needed, it happens in a separate working copy. The coordinating agent checks the result and tests before applying it.

Sol is also the default model for delegated workers. That does not mean an error automatically launches Sol into an unlimited repair loop. Existing error notifications remain in place, and the coordinator decides which investigation to delegate.

## Luna: preparing evidence for judgment

Luna organizes facts, sources, dates, and uncertainty from supplied documents. Its scope excludes filling in missing numbers or turning a factual summary into an investment recommendation. Luna also handles auxiliary work such as session titles.

If an announcement says revenue increased without giving a percentage, the summary should not invent a growth rate. A short account of what the source says and what remains unknown becomes input for the next judgment.

Not every dataset passes through Luna. Sorting a CSV or aggregating profit and loss still belongs in code when the rules are clear.

## How agents hand work to one another

The current collaboration pattern centers on the main conversation. It sends a task and the material needed to complete it, rather than copying the entire conversation to every agent.

The local role runner selects `research`, `operations`, or `organize`, fixes the model, available toolsets, time budget, and turn limit, then starts a fresh CLI session. Native delegation defaults to Sol. Work that needs Astra or Luna uses the corresponding explicit role route. The default delegation call is not an automatic classifier that chooses among all three models.

The prompt separates instructions from reference material. A sentence in a supplied document that looks like a command is not supposed to become part of the assigned task. The runner saves the output, error output, exit status, requested model, and other execution metadata.

The following illustrates a handoff. It is not an actual incident record or an enforced message schema.

```text
Coordinator -> Sol
  Task: narrow down a market-data collection failure
  Material: relevant error logs and the input format
  Restrictions: do not modify the original ledger, strategy,
                or financial execution service
  Return: observed facts, unresolved causes, and the next check

Sol -> Coordinator
  Distinguish observations from possible explanations
  Include the results and limits of any checks performed

Coordinator
  Compare the answer with actual logs, files, and tests
  Handle any repair separately within the approved scope
```

Research follows the same principle. Luna can organize material for Astra to review when needed, but there is no daily automatic Luna-to-Sol-to-Astra pipeline. The role runner also prohibits nested model calls and automatic escalation to a more expensive model.

**A process exiting successfully is not the same as a task being correct.** If a delegated agent reports that tests passed, the coordinator checks the actual execution evidence. The requested model is checked against session and usage records rather than inferred from a runner receipt alone.

Tool lists and prompt restrictions are not an OS sandbox. Roles that can use files and a terminal assume a trusted local worker. Giving roles different names does not create a strong security boundary.

## When the market opens, code does the recurring work

Collection, fixed strategy calculations, risk checks, ledger updates, and the template-based daily report run as `no_agent` scheduled jobs in the current paper system. These jobs run ordinary programs without calling an LLM in that recurring path. Development, research, exception analysis, and month-end interpretation still consume model usage.

The code checks trading dates and session times, collects data, and applies the fixed rules. The strategy currently under observation is a low-frequency trend strategy, so it does not need to trade every day. Distinguishing a no-trade day from missing data or a failed run is part of operating it.

Results go into the ledger and a Korean daily summary scheduled for 09:00 Asia/Seoul. Routine fills are grouped rather than sent as individual overnight messages; urgent safety issues and failures retain a separate notification path. Verified schedules and local runs do not guarantee that every future delivery will succeed.

Month-end work interprets those records: returns and losses, costs, risk, decision quality, and proposed experiments. The intermediate review is assigned to Sol and the final strategy review to Astra. A month-end report is not a signal to start live trading automatically the following month.

## Paper trading has more than one purpose

Combining different purposes under the word "paper" makes results easy to misread. The system distinguishes these tracks:

- The current forward paper episode observes the strategy and operating process as market time passes. Funds and fills are simulated.
- A same-strategy shadow track is designed to receive the same strategy decision as future live trading, then calculate fills and maintain its ledger independently. It compares the effects of execution conditions and costs.
- An alternative-strategy experiment tests a changed rule in a separate ledger. It studies strategy differences rather than execution differences.

The live and shadow paths share versioned strategy decisions and data contracts. They keep capital, fills, ledgers, and stop state separate. Live fills are not copied into the paper ledger to make the results match. A successful alternative experiment is not automatically promoted into the operating strategy either.

The dual-track connection has been implemented, installed, and tested in an isolated environment. Live comparison remains pending until actual opening balances, cost and settlement evidence, and the production data supply are aligned. Replaying historical data does not count as new forward performance.

## Model costs need limits and evidence too

The current configuration bounds role calls by time and turns and limits native delegated workers to two concurrent children. It passes only the required material and does not automatically move to a more expensive model after a failure. Less visible auxiliary calls, including title generation, are included when checking usage.

This is not a budget mechanism that enforces a hard daily token or currency cap. The relative weights of models in the subscription allowance and the actual savings percentage have not been verified. What I can claim is narrower: model calls have been excluded from recurring operations, and the scope of work that uses AI has been limited.

## What has been checked, and what comes next

The implementation records confirm actual calls to the three role models, Sol as the default delegate, and preservation of existing settings and schedules within the approved change scope. The paper/live connection underwent offline testing and installation verification. These checks support the role routing and software execution paths.

They do not establish live-account performance or profitability. Live trading has not been activated. Actual capital, the final strategy, and the account baseline still need to be fixed before a separate operational validation. A short paper episode cannot establish long-term returns either.

The next decision depends less on adding features than on what the paper run teaches us. I want to check whether records remain consistent through missing data and restarts, whether results can be explained after costs, and whether there is evidence to keep or change the strategy.

AI handles part of the research and interpretation. Code handles recurring execution, verification stands before changes, and a person retains responsibility for investment direction and live activation. I have paid more attention to where each role's responsibility ends than to how many agents the system contains.
